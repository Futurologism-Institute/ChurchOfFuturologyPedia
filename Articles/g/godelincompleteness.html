Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorems

First Incompleteness Theorem
Any consistent formal system F that is sufficiently powerful to represent basic arithmetic (contains Robinson arithmetic Q or Peano arithmetic) is incomplete: there exists a sentence G_F such that
F ⊬ G_F and F ⊬ ¬G_F.
(G_F can be constructed to say “I am not provable in F”.)
Second Incompleteness Theorem
Such a system F cannot prove its own consistency:
F ⊬ Con(F)
where Con(F) formalizes “there is no proof of 0=1 in F”.

Key techniques: Gödel numbering (arithmetization of syntax), diagonal lemma / fixed-point lemma.
