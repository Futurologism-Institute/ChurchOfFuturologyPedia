Transhumanism — Symbolic Formalization

Core claims of the contemporary transhumanist movement formalized as a system of predicates and modal statements.

1. Primitive Predicates

  • Human(x) — x is a present-day biological human.
  • Enhanced(x, t) — x has undergone technological enhancement at time t.
  • Posthuman(x) — capacities of x strictly exceed those of any present human.
  • Tech(τ) — τ is an enhancement technology (NBIC: nano, bio, info, cogno).
  • ExistentialRisk(r) — r threatens the survival of Earth-originating intelligent life.

2. Core Axioms / Commitments

Possibility of Enhancement
∀x (Human(x) → ◇∃τ (Tech(τ) ∧ Enhanced(x, τ)))
“It is possible for any human to be enhanced by some technology.”
Desirability of Enhancement
∀x (Human(x) → Prefer(Enhanced(x), ¬Enhanced(x)))
(where Prefer is a preference relation over future states)
Posthuman Trajectory
∃ path π such that Human →_π Posthuman
There exists a continuous (or stepwise) technological trajectory from human to posthuman.
Existential Risk Mitigation
∀r (ExistentialRisk(r) → Ought(Minimize(r)))

3. Definition of Transhumanism (Formal)

Transhumanism ≜
the class of philosophies that affirm both the possibility and the desirability of the axioms above, together with the ethical study of the technologies that realize them.

4. Relation to Posthumanism

Transhumanism ⊂ Activist-Posthumanism in most taxonomies (Bostrom, More, Sorgner). The key distinction is the retention of a continuous developmental path rather than a radical ontological rupture.
