Laws of Thermodynamics

Zeroth Law
If A is in thermal equilibrium with B and B with C, then A is in thermal equilibrium with C.
(Defines temperature as an equivalence relation.)
First Law
ΔU = Q − W
(Internal energy is a state function; energy is conserved.)
Second Law (Clausius / Kelvin statements + entropy)
For any irreversible process in an isolated system, ΔS > 0.
For a reversible process dS = đQ_rev / T.
Third Law (Nernst)
As T → 0, the entropy of a perfect crystal approaches a constant (often taken as zero).
